# CISSP Study Plan

## Phase 1 — Baseline

Cover all eight domains once. The objective is not mastery; it is to build a mental map of the CBK and identify unfamiliar areas.

Track topics as:

- Strong
- Needs revision
- Weak / unfamiliar

## Phase 2 — Question-driven learning

Start mixed practice questions and maintain an error log.

For every error, record:

| Error type | What it means |
|---|---|
| Knowledge | I did not know the concept |
| Confusion | I mixed up similar concepts |
| Mindset | I chose a technical answer over the required management/process answer |
| Sequencing | I knew the actions but chose the wrong FIRST step |
| Reading | I missed a qualifier or detail |

The goal is to reduce recurring error types, not merely increase the question count.

## Phase 3 — Weak-domain drilling

Allocate most study time to repeated weaknesses. Use focused questions and short revision sessions until you can explain the concept without relying on the answer choices.

## Phase 4 — Mixed simulation

Return to mixed-domain questions. Practise switching rapidly between governance, networking, architecture, IAM, operations and software-security contexts.

Focus on reasoning quality and consistency.

## Final days

Prioritise:

- recurring weak concepts;
- mnemonics and easily confused models;
- process sequences;
- risk/governance responsibilities;
- BCP/DR concepts;
- IAM;
- crypto fundamentals;
- architecture/network fundamentals; and
- SDLC/security concepts.

Avoid introducing large amounts of completely new material at the last minute.

## Exam day

- Arrive with enough time to settle in.
- Read qualifiers carefully.
- Don't let one difficult question affect the next one.
- Do not try to reverse-engineer the adaptive exam.
- Answer the question in front of you.
- Prefer sound security/risk reasoning over unnecessary technical complexity.
