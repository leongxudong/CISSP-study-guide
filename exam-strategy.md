# CISSP Exam Strategy

## Think at the right level

A common CISSP trap is selecting a technically correct action that is not the **best organisational action**.

Before answering, identify the level of the question:

- Governance — accountability, policy, risk ownership, business objectives
- Management — programme design, prioritisation, resources, lifecycle
- Operational — procedures, response, administration
- Technical — configuration, protocols, architecture, mechanisms

Answer at the level the question asks.

## FIRST questions

When asked what to do first, resist jumping straight to remediation. Typical prerequisites may include:

1. Protecting human safety.
2. Understanding the situation or requirement.
3. Following policy and established procedure.
4. Assessing risk/impact.
5. Obtaining appropriate authority.
6. Implementing the control.

The exact sequence depends on the scenario; this is a reasoning framework, not a universal rule.

## BEST / MOST questions

Several answers may be true. Look for the option that:

- most directly addresses the stated objective;
- addresses root cause rather than one symptom;
- is consistent with risk management and governance;
- provides the most complete lifecycle/process where appropriate; and
- does not assume facts absent from the question.

## The manager-versus-engineer trap

If the scenario asks what a security manager should do, don't automatically choose the tool or configuration you would personally implement as an engineer.

Examples of higher-level considerations include:

- business impact;
- risk assessment;
- policy;
- legal/regulatory obligations;
- ownership and accountability;
- approval;
- cost-benefit; and
- lifecycle management.

## Risk ownership

Security professionals identify, analyse, communicate and help treat risk. Acceptance of material business risk belongs to the appropriately authorised business/risk owner.

## During the exam

- Read the final sentence carefully.
- Identify the qualifier: FIRST, BEST, MOST, PRIMARY, LEAST.
- Eliminate obviously incorrect choices.
- Compare the remaining answers against the level and objective of the question.
- Avoid adding assumptions.
- Make the best decision with the information provided and move on.

## Practice correctly

A practice question is useful when you can explain:

1. why the correct answer is correct;
2. why each alternative is weaker; and
3. what clue in the question distinguishes them.

That is closer to CISSP reasoning than memorising answer keys.
